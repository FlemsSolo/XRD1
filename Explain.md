1. What is AR/VR/AV/MR/XR and how do the terminologies compare?
• XR (Extended Reality) is the umbrella term covering all technologies that blend or
replace reality with digital content.
• VR (Virtual Reality) completely replaces the user's view of the real world with a fully
digital environment.
o Example: Meta Quest VR games, VR flight simulators.
• AR (Augmented Reality) overlays digital objects onto the real world while the user
still sees their surroundings.
o Example: Pokémon GO, IKEA Place.
• MR (Mixed Reality) blends real and virtual content so that digital objects can
interact with the physical environment.
o Example: A virtual object appearing anchored to a real desk and hidden
behind real objects.
• AV (Augmented Virtuality) is less common and sits between AR and VR.
o Mostly virtual environments enriched with real-world data (e.g., a virtual
meeting room containing live video feeds of participants).
• Reality-Virtuality Continuum
o Real World → AR → MR → AV → VR
• Key difference
o AR enhances reality.
o MR merges reality and digital content.
o VR replaces reality.
o XR includes them all.
2. How do XR applications compare to traditional desktop and mobile
applications?
Advantages of XR
• More immersive and engaging experiences.
• Better spatial understanding of 3D data.
• Natural interaction through gaze, hand tracking, gestures, voice, and body
movement.
• Improved learning and retention for many practical training tasks.
• Enables simulation of dangerous, expensive, or impossible scenarios.
• More intuitive collaboration around 3D content.
Disadvantages of XR
• Higher hardware costs.
• Greater application complexity and development effort.
• User discomfort such as motion sickness.
• Battery and performance limitations.
• Reduced portability compared to smartphones.
• Potential social isolation in fully immersive VR environments.

4. How does XR add value to the end user?
• Makes information more context-aware and easier to understand.
• Provides hands-on learning without real-world risks.
• Reduces travel requirements through virtual collaboration.
• Improves task efficiency with guided workflows.
• Enhances entertainment experiences through immersion.
• Supports accessibility with alternative interaction methods.
• Enables experiences that would otherwise be impossible, dangerous, or expensive.
• Improves memory retention by leveraging spatial and experiential learning.

• Technician receives repair instructions directly on machinery.
• Student explores the human body in 3D.
• Architect walks through a future building before construction.
• Customer tries furniture virtually before purchasing.
5. How does current XR technology compare to an idealistic future
vision?
Current XR Technology
• Headsets are still relatively bulky.
• Limited battery life.
• Restricted field of view.
• High-end devices remain expensive.
• Tracking occasionally loses accuracy.
• Social acceptance is still evolving.
• Mixed reality occlusion and realism are imperfect.
Future Vision
• Lightweight glasses replacing headsets.
• All-day battery life.
• Near-human field of view.
• Photorealistic graphics.
• Perfect environmental understanding.
• Seamless integration with AI assistants.
• Persistent digital worlds shared by multiple users.
• Natural interaction using eye movement, voice, and subtle gestures.
• Possible brain-computer interfaces for direct interaction.
Gap to Close
• Display technology
• Battery technology
• Comfort and ergonomics
• Computing power
• Network infrastructure
• User acceptance and privacy concerns
6. How does immersion and presence factor into XR?
Immersion
Immersion refers to the degree the technology can convincingly surround the user's senses.
Factors affecting immersion:
• Display quality
• Wide field of view
• Spatial audio
• Low latency
• Realistic graphics
• Natural interactions
Presence
Presence is the psychological feeling of "being there."
Relationship
• High immersion often increases presence.
• Presence is ultimately the user's subjective perception.
• A highly immersive system does not always guarantee presence if interactions feel
unnatural.
Why It Matters
Greater presence often leads to:
• Better learning
• Increased engagement
• Stronger emotional impact
• Improved training outcomes
• Better collaboration experiences
